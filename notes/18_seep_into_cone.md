# Note 18 — Seep into the cone

**Date:** 2026-10-09
**Status:** Ledger opened. Differential seep not generated. Step 1 stays closed.

A shared audit scale is not a shared physical mechanism.

## Opening the ledger

The transferred packet keeps norm 1 inside the near-zero pair. That is a closed projection, not a closed world. Energy can leave the pair if the pair is coupled to something else. The something already on the record is the cone scalar.

The n=1 radial equation, version 4 notes 60 and 62, is

(1/r) d/dr ( r d phi/dr ) = dV/dphi - J.

Integrate across an interval. The flux jumps by the enclosed source,

[ r phi' ] = integral (dV/dphi - J) r dr.

The power leaving the source into the scalar is the work P = integral J (d phi / dt) r dr. That is the seep. Total energy of packet plus cone can still balance. The two-mode norm no longer has to.

## What the cone actually does

Massless, no source between two radii: r phi' = C at both. The flux is the same. There is no differential seep between them.

A source between the walls makes the flux jump by the enclosed J. A symmetric source, the packet with even m, sends equal flux to the two sides. The odd part is zero. Note 11 still applies: an even leak is a common shift, not a sigma_z detuning.

A massive cone scalar replaces the constant flux by a K1 tail, version 4 note 61. mu is inserted. The tail decays. It does not pick a left wall over a right wall. No pause, phi' = 0, appeared in those runs.

## Identification not made

Putting the two walls at different cone radii would make the flux differ. That identifies the kink coordinate x with the cone radius r. Neither reduction forces that identification. The cone solve does not contain m(x). The kink Hamiltonian does not contain r.

## Result

The packet ledger can be opened. The seep is the cone work term, if J is the packet. A symmetric packet still cannot tilt itself. Step 1 stays closed.
