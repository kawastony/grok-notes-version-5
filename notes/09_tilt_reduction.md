## Import

The isolated packet does not contain g. The smallest gravitational coupling of a Dirac fermion is the Newtonian redshift, H → H + Phi(x). This is an identification, not a reduction of the kink Hamiltonian. It is socket 3 of note 4, written in, not derived.

Constant acceleration: Phi = -g x. Walls at +d and -d. The potential difference is 2 g d. In the left/right basis of note 2,

H_2 = (Delta E / 2) sigma_x + (g d) sigma_z.

The gap is sqrt(Delta E^2 + (2 g d)^2). The ground-state imbalance between the walls is

x / sqrt(1 + x^2), x = 2 g d / Delta E.

## What matches

That imbalance is the standard MOND function mu(x) = x / sqrt(1+x^2), if x is read as g/g_c with

g_c = Delta E / (2 d) = A(v) exp(-2 v d) / (2 d).

The simple interpolator x/(1+x) is not this object. At x = 1 the standard value is 0.707 and the simple value is 0.5. The identification of imbalance with mu is a reading, not a forced map from g_N to g_obs. Version 4 already recorded that galaxy fits prefer the slower simple and RAR joins, and that solar-system tests prefer a faster return. This reduction picks the faster join, and only after the import.

## What does not match

A(1) = 2. Then g_c(d=1) / g_c(d=3) = 164 at v = 1. At v = 2 the same ratio is about 8900. The earlier lattice combination d (Delta E)^2 varied by about a thousand between those separations; this critical acceleration varies by 164. Either way it is not one number.

No minimum over d selects a preferred packet. A tension term linear in d drives the separation to the edge of the scan. The fermion piece does not stabilize a universal g_c.

The functional form of the imbalance is grade 1 inside the imported two-level problem. The step that inserts Phi is chosen. g_c is grade 3: it runs with v and d, and no measurement inside the packet fixes it. This is not a derivation of a0.
